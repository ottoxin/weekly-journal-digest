#### American Journal of Political Science

- **Partisan bias in a sorted party system: A problem of epistemic inequality**
  Published: 2026-08-26
  Authors: Justin Pottle
  Affiliations: Department of Political Science Loyola University Chicago Chicago Illinois USA
  DOI: 10.1111/ajps.70086
  Link: https://doi.org/10.1111/ajps.70086
  Abstract: Abstract Normative and empirical scholars typically treat partisans’ distrust of their political rivals as a product of individual cognitive biases. But even if bias is inevitable, it is parties’ coalition‐building and messaging strategies that determine who bias is directed toward. This paper argues that the social sorting of party conflict exacerbates the epistemic and democratic harm of partisan distrust by generating distinctive patterns of epistemic inequality. I show that when voters associate parties with particular social groups, members of those groups suffer greater risk of testimonial injustice and related credibility deficits owing to their perceived partisanship. These dynamics are most damaging for members of marginalized groups, as party–group overlap can reinforce the prejudices they already face. Addressing these harms, I argue, requires looking beyond individual partisans to the background features of party systems that shape how elites mobilize group identities and animus in the struggle for office.

- **Less Effort to Believe and More Effort to Disbelieve: Examining the Interplay between News-Finds-Me and Affective Polarization in Political Fake News Susceptibility in the United States**
  Published: 2026-08-26
  Authors: Yu Tian; Lars Willnat; Baohua Zhou
  Affiliations: Fudan University, Shanghai, P. R. China; Syracuse University, Syracuse, NY, USA
  DOI: 10.1177/00936502261472023
  Link: https://doi.org/10.1177/00936502261472023
  Abstract: Drawing upon an online survey of 719 US adult partisans incorporating response latency data, we connected News-Finds-Me (NFM) to the partisan-motivated reasoning literature and theorized it as a consensus-based mode of news exposure that leads to different news-processing pathways, where the vested news-processing effort depends on whether the political falsehood aligns with one’s partisan identity and surrounding majority opinion. Individual-level analysis and headline-level modeling jointly illuminated the asymmetric interplay between NFM and affective polarization. In partisan-consistent scenarios, high-NFM users devoted limited processing time, falling short of cognitive effort to enable affect-laden, directional believing of partisan-consistent fake news. However, when challenged by partisan inconsistency, high-NFM users dedicated significantly more processing time, which exacerbated the relationship between affective polarization and refutation of partisan-inconsistent falsehoods. Contrarily, such an effort discrepancy, widely documented in the partisan-motivated reasoning literature, was weakened or reversed for low-NFM individuals. Extending the “Effort-Reinforces-Directional-Reasoning” thesis to fake news vulnerability, our findings underscore the paradoxical interplay between NFM and affective polarization: while NFM and affective polarization deviate people from active news-seeking and accuracy-oriented reasoning, their joint dynamics form a type of group thinking that reduces vulnerability to partisan falsehoods—even those aligned with one’s partisan attachments.

- **From Exposure to Creation: Cultivating Narrative Ownership to Reduce Stigma**
  Published: 2026-08-24
  Authors: Xun Zhu
  Affiliations: University of Kentucky, Lexington, USA
  DOI: 10.1177/00936502261478972
  Link: https://doi.org/10.1177/00936502261478972
  Abstract: Stories are powerful agents of attitude change, including attitudes toward stigmatized groups. However, existing theories have focused on narrative exposure, with limited attention to narrative creation. Creating a story involves simulating characters and events and expressing them through language, which may engage unique psychological processes. This research investigates how and why creating stories shapes stigma. In three experiments, participants wrote, imagined, or read a positive story about a stigmatized character. Study 1 showed that narrative creation, especially writing, increased psychological ownership, which predicted greater transportation and lower stigma. Study 2 replicated these findings using a different stigmatized group. Study 3 showed that psychological ownership continued to explain the effects of narrative creation above and beyond perceived agency. These findings extend theories of narrative persuasion by considering the effects of story creation on intergroup attitudes and introducing psychological ownership as a novel mechanism linking creation to narrative processing and outcomes.

- **Agrivoltaics can reduce political polarization and local opposition to solar energy on land**
  Published: 2026-08-27

  DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-77141-8

  Abstract: Abstract As solar energy deployment on land intensifies, political polarization and local opposition increasingly challenge its social acceptance. We propose that agrivoltaics – a dual land-use technology that combines solar power generation with agricultural production – can help bridge ideological divides and reduce local opposition by minimizing land-use conflicts and offering additional benefits to rural communities. - **Multi-source agent-based modeling to optimize influenza mitigation strategies in Hong Kong**
  Published: 2026-08-25
  Authors: Liping Peng; Yiyang Guo; Nicole N. Y. Tsang; Xiaotong Huang; Hau Chi So; Benjamin J. Cowling; Dennis K. M. Ip; Tim K. Tsang
  DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-74857-5
  Link: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-74857-5
  Abstract: Seasonal influenza control faces challenges from variable vaccine effectiveness and uncertain non-pharmaceutical intervention (NPI) performance. While vaccination remains the primary strategy, its effectiveness varies between vaccine-matched and mismatched seasons. Increased post-COVID-19 NPI acceptance enhances influenza control feasibility, yet optimal combination approaches remain poorly understood. We use an agent-based model to analyze influenza transmission across six seasons in Hong Kong (2009-2013) with varying epidemic characteristics. We integrate surveillance, serological, and school absenteeism data for calibration, enabling accurate estimation of reported and unreported infections. We evaluate age-targeted vaccination, staying home when sick, mask use, and school-based interventions across diverse real-world scenarios. Compared to baseline, child vaccination consistently outperforms other strategies, with targeting those under 12 yielding the greatest population-level attack rate reduction (up to 8.5% relative reduction per 100,000 vaccinated). Among NPIs, 40% mask coverage reduces attack rates by 18%–43%, comparable to 25% of symptomatic individuals staying home. During vaccine-mismatched seasons, combining high-coverage mask use and staying home reduces attack rates by 79%–84%. High-coverage school-based vaccination is more effective than closures, reducing student attack rates by up to 85% versus 33% for 14-day closures. Our multi-source calibration approach provides robust evidence for prioritizing child vaccination and strategic NPI combinations. Modeling six influenza seasons to evaluate mitigation strategies, this study proposes a signal-responsive policy framework. Findings show child vaccination and mask use curb transmission; their combination is vital during vaccine mismatches.

  Abstract: How does the ever-growing relevance of social media shape political campaigning?   Abstract: Climate change is intensifying coastal flooding, riverine flooding and tropical cyclones, yet risks to telecommunications infrastructure remain poorly quantified despite growing societal dependence on digital connectivity. We present a global, event-based geospatial assessment of mobile cellular infrastructure vulnerability, combining open crowdsourced asset data with global climate hazard models under historical conditions and future scenarios, RCP4.5 and RCP8.5. We analyse approximately 17 million mobile basestations worldwide, estimating exposure and direct damage across extreme-event return periods. Under RCP8.5 in 2050, a 0.1% annual probability tropical cyclone event affects an estimated 3.7 million basestations, causing USD 4.22 billion in direct damage. Under RCP8.5 in 2080, equivalent coastal flooding affects 268,000 basestations, causing USD 6.44 billion in damage. Riverine flooding affects more assets globally, approximately 2.8 million, but damages remain broadly stable at USD 46–47 billion. Telecommunications infrastructure assets should therefore be systematically incorporated into climate risk assessments. Mobile networks face growing risks from climate hazards. The study finds that floods and tropical cyclones could affect millions of base stations worldwide and cause billions of dollars in damage.

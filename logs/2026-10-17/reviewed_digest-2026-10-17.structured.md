- **Spatiality of preemptive coercion: How autocrats demolish informal settlements for political control**
  Published: 2026-10-14
  Authors: Shiqi Ma
  Affiliations: Department of Politics and International Studies SOAS University of London London United Kingdom
  DOI: 10.1111/ajps.70085
  Link: https://doi.org/10.1111/ajps.70085
  Abstract: Abstract Why do authorities demolish informal settlements in metropolises? The social sciences have a long tradition of addressing this issue through the lens of economic development or welfare provision. I instead propose a theory of political control. Autocrats adopt demolition as a preemptive and coercive tool to remove communities substantively and symbolically less legible to them, thereby ensuring societal compliance and restoring visual order. Empirically, I compile a unique dataset of 320 demolished and undemolished informal settlements in Beijing between 2017 and 2018, based on satellite imagery, digital street views, and fieldwork. The results indicate that the low legibility of informal settlements to authorities correlates with a greater likelihood of demolition. This paper contributes to the social science literature by connecting legibility politics with urban governance. It also introduces a spatial perspective and leverages novel data sources to deepen our understanding of preemptive coercion under authoritarianism.

- **Public Service Decline and Support for the Populist Right: Evidence from England’s National Health Service**
  Published: 2026-10-13
  Authors: ZACHARY P. DICKSON; SARA B. HOBOLT; CATHERINE DE VRIES; SIMONE CREMASCHI
  Affiliations: IE University; London School of Economics and Political Science; University of Oxford
  DOI: 10.1017/s0003055426101944
  Link: https://doi.org/10.1017/s0003055426101944
  Abstract: The rise of populist right parties is well studied, but relatively little attention has been given to how public service performance influences voter support. Given that public services are often the primary means through which citizens interact with the state, we argue that declining public services can create grievances that increase the appeal of populist right parties. Focusing on England’s National Health Service, we combine administrative data on local health facility closures with panel data on public preferences and voting intentions. Our findings underscore the role of public service decline as a driver of support for populist parties, especially in areas undergoing demographic change.

- **Two Paths of Conversation: How Stories and Statistics are Associated With Community Engagement in Online Debate Surrounding Vaccine Science Skeptical Content**
  Published: 2026-10-17
  Authors: Han Li; Inhwan Bae; Pengfei Zhao; Natalie Bazarova; Drew Margolin
  Affiliations: Cornell University, Ithaca, NY, USA; Florida State University, Tallahassee, FL, USA
  DOI: 10.1177/00936502261496144
  Link: https://doi.org/10.1177/00936502261496144
  Abstract: Statistical and narrative evidence represent two distinct ways of substantiating claims in public reasoning, but does this distinction matter for how people engage with vaccine science skeptical (VSS) content and the discursive spaces surrounding it? This study addresses this question through a large-scale, LLM-assisted observational analysis of online engagement with VSS posts and comments that support or counter such content using statistical versus narrative evidence in the Reddit community r/DebateVaccines . Building on Fisher’s distinction between rational and narrative paradigms , we theorize two paths of conversation through which evidentiary forms are associated with audience engagement with contentious vaccine discourses in a naturalistic social media setting: a rational pathway, marked by concentrated participation and corrective contestation around statistical claims and counterclaims, tending toward a single evidence-based truth “approved” by one-click reactions; and a narrative pathway, characterized by generative engagement featuring broader and more distributed participation, more discursive and generative engagement, and greater acceptance of competing viewpoints. These findings provide a springboard for theorizing evidentiary form as a key epistemic logic associated with how conversations evolve in real-world engagement with contentious, socially consequential issues such as vaccine misinformation, while informing hybrid approaches to addressing skepticism toward established vaccine science.

#### Nature

- **Educational intervention can rewire village social networks**
  Published: 2026-10-14
  Authors: Marios Papamichalis; Laura Forastiere; Edoardo M. Airoldi; Nicholas A. Christakis
  DOI: 10.1038/s41586-026-11154-7
  Link: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11154-7
  Abstract: Social networks shape behaviour, affect information dissemination and undergird collective action. And they can be exploited in the design of interventions in these domains1-7. Although social networks obey fundamental principles, such as the degree distribution8,9, they can nevertheless evolve as ties form and break. Here, through a randomized controlled trial involving a total of 8,331 people in 110 remote Honduran villages, we evaluate the secondary effects of a 22-month public-health intervention1 on pre-existing social-network structure. We used a design in which a varying fraction of village households received the intervention, and villages in which no one received the intervention were used as the primary reference group. In 'low-dosage' villages (in which 5-30% of households received the intervention), people who received the intervention tended to sever ties with untreated individuals to whom they previously went for health advice. Conversely, in high-dosage villages (50-100%), treated individuals increased their ties. These average changes are explained mainly by tie dynamics; that is, treated-treated pairs persist and form new links, whereas treated-untreated ties dissolve. A mediation analysis indicates that absorption of health information increases the likelihood of tie formation and persistence. The rewiring patterns generally lasted for three years after the intervention ceased. Moreover, although the intervention delivered health information, it reshaped friendship and financial ties, not just health-advice connections. In aggregate, the introduction of a new information regime acted as a kind of social institution, rewiring individuals' connections even though the overall degree distribution of village-wide networks remained relatively stable.

  DOI: 10.1017/pan.2026.10057
  Link: https://doi.org/10.1017/pan.2026.10057
  Abstract: Abstract Annotated text data are central to many empirical applications in political science, yet many annotation tasks involve categories that are sparsely populated. These low- N categories pose problems for both encoder-only and decoder-only (generative) Transformer large language models (LLMs), leading to low performance, biased evaluation and measurement error in downstream analyses. This article introduces the synthetic imputation approach, a targeted method for expanding sparse categories while remaining consistent with the category-defining content of the human-coded examples.

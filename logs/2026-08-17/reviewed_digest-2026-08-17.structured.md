- **Affective Legitimacy and Digital Populism: Evidence from Romania’s Annulled Presidential Elections**
  Published: 2026-08-13
  Authors: Antonio Amuza; Vasile Dîncu
  Affiliations: University of Bucharest
  DOI: 10.1080/10584609.2026.2714818
  Link: https://doi.org/10.1080/10584609.2026.2714818
  Abstract: On 6 December 2024, Romania’s Constitutional Court annulled the first round of presidential elections, triggering an exceptional crisis of democratic legitimacy in post-communist Europe. While procedurally justified, the decision sparked intense public reactions, both emotional and political. The findings confirm that populist interpretations of institutional actions proliferate in fragmented and emotionalized digital media environments, where distrust is personalized and intensified. Drawing on a nationally representative survey (N = 964), conducted by IRES shortly after the annulment, the study examines how Romanian citizens perceived the legitimacy of the Court’s decision and identifies the key socio-political and media-related factors shaping these perceptions. Binary logistic regression and mediation models were used to assess the drivers of perceived illegitimacy. TikTok use, negative emotions (anger, disappointment, resentment), political distrust, and anti-establishment party support significantly increase the likelihood of viewing the decision as illegitimate, whereas trust in journalists reduces it. The study contributes to the literature on populism as a communicative style and emotional register, showing how legitimacy itself becomes a contested symbol in digital environments marked by personalization, fragmentation, and affective intensification. We argue that institutional legitimacy is no longer grounded solely in procedural legality but is increasingly shaped by emotionally resonant narratives circulating through digital platforms. We complement the survey with a headline-level media analysis (N = 3,923; Dec 2024 - Aug 2025), to serve as a contextual counterfactual: instead of escalating antagonism, mainstream media gradually stabilize institutional language. A keyness comparison shows that the Early window (first 14 days) is driven by institutional/legal terms (CCR, decision, annulment), while the Late window becomes actor-centric (Simion, Antonescu, Ponta, Bolojan). Over time, lexical entropy declines markedly, signaling a standardization of vocabulary around procedural frames rather than escalating antagonism. Taken together, the results point to a decoupling between media normalization and individual affect, suggesting that democratic legitimacy in platformized communication environments is increasingly produced through affective legitimacy rather than procedural authority.

- **Entertainment as Trojan Horse: Voluntary Consumption of Propaganda in China**
  Published: 2026-08-13
  Authors: Hanzhang Liu; Linan Yao
  Affiliations: Florida State University; Vanderbilt University
  DOI: 10.1080/10584609.2026.2701201
  Link: https://doi.org/10.1080/10584609.2026.2701201
  Abstract: For authoritarian propaganda to change hearts and minds, it must first reach the masses and capture their attention. In an era of media proliferation, what can an authoritarian regime do to make ordinary citizens consume pro-regime propaganda voluntarily? How can it lower citizens’ resistance to being indoctrinated? In this article, we answer these questions by studying a new generation of propaganda movies in contemporary China. Leveraging observational data and a pre-registered survey experiment, we find that entertainment elements in propaganda movies – both personality-based and narrative-based – play a key role in appealing to viewers and increasing consumption; this effect is especially pronounced among groups that are the least inclined to expose themselves to propaganda in the first place. Also, viewers are more receptive to political indoctrination when it is interwoven with entertainment, even when they recognize such content. Combined, these findings highlight the importance of entertainment in authoritarian propaganda: not only does it serve as a vehicle to disseminate political indoctrination, but it also functions as a source of appeal that renders such indoctrination more palatable to the masses.

- **Kamala is Brat: TikTok and the Affective Politics of Memes**
  Published: 2026-08-12
  Authors: Laura Cervi; Sarah J. Jackson
  Affiliations: Autonomous University of Barcelona; University of Pennsylvania
  DOI: 10.1080/10584609.2026.2715697
  Link: https://doi.org/10.1080/10584609.2026.2715697
  Abstract: In this article, we examine TikTok memes about former Vice president Kamala Harris that circulated through the digital registers of the Charli XCX-inspired “Kamala is Brat” trend. By analyzing the form, function, and affective resonance of these memes, we explore how creators interpreted and reframed Harris’s image leading up to the 2024 U.S. presidential election. Using a multimodal analysis, we introduce a three-layer analytic that distinguishes memetic form, communicative function, and affective character. Our findings show how creators mobilized recognizable meme templates, narrative positioning, and affective cues to recast Harris through identity-signaling and aesthetic contrast to her opponent. We also find that TikTok’s features enabled a pop culture intertextuality in which other viral trends – including the appeal of “it girl” Julia Fox, and the symbolic ambiguity of signifiers ranging from Pulp Fiction, The Matrix, and coconuts – become linked to the candidate. We argue that platform-native meme cultures on TikTok operate as mechanisms of vernacular electoral mediation, enabling creators to reinterpret candidate imagery and political narratives through the aesthetic conventions of platform culture. In doing so, this work demonstrates how participatory meme practices contribute to the symbolic construction of electoral candidates in contemporary digital media environments.

- **Trending Topics, Growing Knowledge? How Curating Actors’ Political Social Media Content Affects Youth’s Political Knowledge**
  Published: 2026-08-10

  DOI: 10.1080/10584609.2026.2708127
  Link: https://doi.org/10.1080/10584609.2026.2708127
  Abstract: Whether youth can gain political knowledge through social media is long debated. Existing research has insufficiently accounted for different curating actors of political information, an indirect learning logic, and within-person changes. To fill these gaps, we conducted a large-scale three-wave panel study among 16- to 30-year-olds (NT3 = 2276) to examine how exposure to different social media actors such as politicians, peers, or influencers relates to the acquisition of current affairs knowledge over time. - **Durable majority gerrymanders: Where partisan gerrymandering can displace democracy**

  Abstract: Abstract We develop the concept of, and estimation tools for, durable majority gerrymanders : electoral district maps drawn to reduce an opposing political party's probability of winning a majority in a legislative chamber. - **Endogenous coalition formation in policy debates**
  Published: 2026-08-10
  Authors: Philip Leifeld; Laurence Brandenberger
  Affiliations: Department of Social Statistics University of Manchester Manchester UK; Institute of Political Science University of Zurich Zurich Switzerland
  DOI: 10.1111/ajps.70079
  Link: https://doi.org/10.1111/ajps.70079
  Abstract: Abstract Political actors form coalitions around their joint normative beliefs in order to influence the policy process on contentious issues such as climate change or population aging. Policy process theory maintains that learning within and across coalitions is a central predictor of policy change but has yet to explain how policy learning works. This article explains the formation and maintenance of coalitions by focusing on the ways actors adopt policy beliefs from other actors in policy debates.

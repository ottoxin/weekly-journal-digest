  Abstract: Abstract Low‐skilled men have long been vulnerable to globalization, decarbonization, and other forms of economic disruption. #### American Political Science Review

- **Structural Injustice as Emergent Disorder**
  Published: 2026-09-25
  Authors: HOCHAN KIM
  Affiliations: Bates College
  DOI: 10.1017/s0003055426101877
  Link: https://doi.org/10.1017/s0003055426101877
  Abstract: Abstract Structural injustice remains a puzzling concept despite its increasing prominence. I distill recent theoretical challenges into a dilemma: either structural injustice lacks the normative credentials of injustice given that wrongdoing is inessential, or it is redundant given existing theories that trace injustice to culpable state and other institutional failures. I then develop a novel account of structural injustice, the Complexity View, that unravels this dilemma. The emergent properties of human actions in a complex society explain what is “structural” about structural injustice, specifically how it can arise without (culpable) wrongdoing. Structural injustice is unjust because it is wrong to contribute to social processes that cause harm and to passively accept preventable forms of vulnerability and deprivation as tragic misfortunes. But this account of structural injustice has a sobering implication: while complexity explains what distinguishes structural injustice from other wrongs, it also sets significant epistemic limits on social reform.

- **The Punitive Dimension of Presidential Particularism**
  Published: 2026-09-24
  Authors: LUKAS K. ALEXANDER
  Affiliations: Washington University in St. Louis
  DOI: 10.1017/s0003055426102020
  Link: https://doi.org/10.1017/s0003055426102020
  Abstract: Existing accounts of presidential particularism emphasize the targeted distribution of benefits to electorally valuable constituencies. This research note demonstrates that presidential particularism can also operate punitively, through the selective imposition of costs on constituencies that provided weaker electoral support for the president. I examine this dynamic through the Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE), a highly centralized and politicized agency created at the start of Donald Trump’s second term. Leveraging data on nearly 700,000 federal contracts linked to DOGE contract cuts and 2024 county-level election returns, I show that these cuts were systematically concentrated in counties that provided weaker electoral support for Trump. These patterns hold across contract- and county-level analyses. Together, these findings broaden theories of presidential particularism by showing that presidents may use administrative authority to impose costs on electorally oppositional constituencies. More broadly, I demonstrate how presidents can wield administrative power as a tool of partisan punishment.

  Abstract: Media that effectively perform their democratic functions are widely considered a key driver of democratization, yet longitudinal evidence on their impact remains limited. This study analyzed global annual time-series data on democratic media performance and democratization from 1946 to 2018. ARIMA models indicated a covariation between annual increases in democratic media performance and gains in democratization worldwide, measured by Vanhanen’s Index of Democratization.   Abstract: Abstract We examine the conditions in which people reject cross-cutting news in naturalistic settings of online comments. - **Disentangling interaction and bias effects in opinion dynamics of large language models**
  Published: 2026-09-22
  Authors: Vincent C. Brockers; David A. Ehrlich; Viola Priesemann
  DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-77340-3
  Link: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77340-3
  Abstract: Abstract Large Language Models are increasingly used to simulate human opinion dynamics, yet the effect of genuine interaction is often obscured by systematic biases. We develop a Bayesian framework to disentangle and quantify three such biases: (i) A topic bias toward the LLM’s default stance; (ii) an agreement bias favoring agreement to the prompted statement irrespective of the question; and (iii) an anchoring bias toward the initiating agent’s stance.
